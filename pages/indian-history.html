A journey across millennia

Indian history

Many peoples, ideas, and kingdoms have shaped the subcontinent. Follow a broad timeline of the encounters and achievements that continue to influence India.

Before the present

A past with many
voices
The history of India is not a single, uninterrupted story. It is a changing landscape of cities and villages, states and empires, languages and faiths. Dates below are broad guides; many societies and traditions flourished at the same time. BCE means “Before Common Era” and CE means “Common Era.”

Seven moments in a long history

Mohanjo-daro

Indus Valley cities

Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, and other settlements reveal skilled urban planning, craft production, and long-distance trade. The script found on seals has not yet been deciphered, so much about this civilization remains unknown.

Taj Mahal

Sultanates, Mughals, and regional powers

The Delhi Sultanates and, from 1526, the Mughal Empire governed large parts of northern India at different times. Their histories include warfare and administration as well as artistic exchange; powerful Deccan and other regional states shaped the same centuries.

Sanchi Stupa

Maurya, Gupta, and connected worlds

The Maurya Empire reached a wide area under Ashoka, who promoted the teaching of dhamma after the Kalinga war. Later, Gupta-era courts supported literature, mathematics, and art, while trade connected the subcontinent with regions beyond it.

Gateway of India

Colonial rule and the freedom movement

East India Company power expanded during the eighteenth century, followed by direct British rule after 1858. Resistance took many forms, including the uprising of 1857 and a mass independence movement that led to freedom in 1947 amid Partition.

Flag-of-India

The Republic of India

India became a republic when its Constitution came into effect on 26 January 1950. The world’s largest democracy continues to evolve through elections, social movements, scientific work, and the contributions of its many communities.

Nagra Style Temple

Powerful regional kingdoms

Dynasties across the subcontinent built distinct political and cultural centers. The Cholas became known for maritime links and monumental temples; Pallava, Rashtrakuta, and many other courts also shaped architecture, language, and regional identities.
